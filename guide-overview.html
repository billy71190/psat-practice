PSAT/NMSQT Overview & Scoring

What the test looks like

The PSAT/NMSQT is a digital test taken at school in the College Board's Bluebook app, usually in October. It takes 2 hours 14 minutes of testing time, with a 10-minute break between the two sections.

Total98134 min
  • Adaptive modules. Everyone gets a similar mix of easy, medium, and hard questions in Module 1. How you do on Module 1 decides whether Module 2 is a harder or an easier version. Doing well on Module 1 opens the door to the highest scores, so take it seriously and don't rush.
  • Reading and Writing uses short passages (about 25–150 words), one question per passage. Questions are grouped by type and go roughly from easier to harder within each group.
  • Math questions go from easiest to hardest within each module. About 75% are multiple choice, and the rest are student-produced response (you type the answer). A Desmos graphing calculator is built in and allowed on the whole Math section, and there is a reference sheet of formulas.
  • Inside a module you can skip around, mark questions for review, and change answers. Once you submit a module (or time runs out), you can't go back to it.
  • There's no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a question blank.

What's tested

Reading and Writing

  • Craft and Structure (≈28%): words in context, text structure & purpose, cross-text connections
  • Information and Ideas (≈26%): central ideas & details, command of evidence (text and data), inferences
  • Standard English Conventions (≈26%): sentence boundaries (punctuation), form, structure, and sense (agreement, tense, etc.)
  • Expression of Ideas (≈20%): transitions, rhetorical synthesis (using notes to meet a goal)

Math

  • Algebra (≈35%): linear equations, inequalities, functions, systems
  • Advanced Math (≈32.5%): quadratics, exponentials, polynomials, equivalent expressions, nonlinear functions
  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis (≈20%): ratios, rates, percentages, units, statistics, probability, data
  • Geometry and Trigonometry (≈12.5%): area & volume, angles & triangles, right-triangle trig, circles

Scores aren't just "percent correct." The digital test uses each question's difficulty and the module you were routed to. That's why the scores on this site are labeled estimates: they're a rough guide for tracking progress, not a prediction of an official score.

Your score report also shows percentiles (the percent of test takers you scored at or above) and benchmarks (scores that suggest you're on track for college readiness in each section).

National Merit and the Selection Index

The "NMSQT" stands for National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test. The National Merit Scholarship Corporation (NMSC) uses this test as the first screen for its scholarship program.

Selection Index = (2 × Reading and Writing score + Math score) ÷ 10 Example: RW 620 and Math 500 → (1,240 + 500) ÷ 10 = 174. Reading and Writing counts twice, so every 10 RW points is worth 2 index points, while 10 Math points is worth 1.
  • Only the junior-year (grade 11) test normally counts for National Merit. If you take it in 10th grade, it's great practice and a preview of where you stand.
  • Each year, about 1.3 million juniors enter the program. About 50,000 high scorers are recognized. Roughly two-thirds of them are named Commended Students, and about 16,000 are named Semifinalists.
  • Semifinalists are the highest-scoring entrants in each state (together, less than 1% of U.S. high school seniors). Because cutoffs are set state by state, the Selection Index needed depends on where you live and changes a little every year.
  • Semifinalists can advance to Finalist by completing an application (grades, an SAT/ACT score that confirms the PSAT result, an essay, and a school recommendation). Scholarships are awarded from the Finalist group.

Details change from year to year, so check the official PSAT/NMSQT Student Guide and the National Merit Scholarship Corporation website.

Other reasons the PSAT matters

  • It's a low-stakes rehearsal for the SAT, which has almost the same format (just a bit longer and harder).
  • Some other scholarship programs use PSAT/NMSQT scores.
  • The score report shows exactly which skills to work on.
