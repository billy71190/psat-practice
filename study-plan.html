4-Week Study Plan

About 30–45 minutes a day, 5 days a week, plus two longer practice-test days. Short, steady practice beats cramming. Adjust the plan around school and activities, and if you miss a day, just pick up where you left off.

Week 1: Find your starting point

Day 1Read the Test Overview. Install the free Bluebook app if you'll use it later.
Day 2 (longer)Take Practice Test 1, timed, in one sitting. Don't worry about the score; this is your baseline.
Day 3Review every Reading & Writing question you missed or guessed on. For each, write one sentence: why was my answer wrong?
Day 4Review every Math question you missed. Re-solve it without looking, then check the explanation.
Day 5Learn the punctuation rules in the Conventions guide. Quick Practice: Conventions.

Week 2: Build core skills

Day 1Algebra guide + Quick Practice: Algebra (10 questions).
Day 2Craft & Structure guide + Quick Practice. Start a list of new vocabulary words.
Day 3Advanced Math guide: factoring, quadratics, exponents. Quick Practice.
Day 4Information & Ideas guide + Quick Practice.
Day 5Desmos day: redo 5 math questions using only the Desmos tricks.

Week 3: Fill the gaps

Day 1Transitions & Rhetorical Synthesis + Quick Practice.
Day 2Data Analysis guide (percents, statistics, probability) + Quick Practice.
Day 3Geometry & Trig guide + Quick Practice. Memorize the formulas that aren't on the reference sheet.
Day 4 (longer)Take an official full-length practice test in Bluebook. It's adaptive, just like the real test.
Day 5Review the Bluebook test. Look at your score report to see which skills cost the most points.

Week 4: Put it together

Day 1 (longer)Take Practice Test 2, timed. Compare with Test 1 in My Results.
Day 2Review Test 2 mistakes. Redo any you got wrong on both tests.
Day 3Targeted practice on your 2 weakest skills, using Quick Practice and the official Student Question Bank.
Day 4Light review: grammar rules table, formula list, transition words. Read the Test-Day Tips.
Day 5Rest. Get your device and bag ready and go to bed on time.
The habit that raises scores most: keep a "mistake log." For each miss, write the skill, why you missed it (didn't know the rule, misread, ran out of time), and what you'll do next time. Reread it before every practice test.
